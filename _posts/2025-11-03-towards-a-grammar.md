Attempting to name what this project has been doing all along — aware that naming may close it prematurely. The vocabulary of incompletion is itself incomplete.

## The Problem of Naming

Every project reaches a point where it needs to say what it is. This is usually the moment when the project ends, or becomes something else. The question is whether naming is always a form of closure, or whether there are ways to name that keep the question open.

## What We've Been Doing

Over the course of seven installments, this project has been moving around a set of related questions:

- What makes a mark readable?
- What is surrendered in the passage from trace to legibility?
- How does the archive shape what we can know?
- What remains when the archive is incomplete?

Each installment has approached these questions from a different angle. But we haven't yet named the project itself. This installment attempts to do that — but with the awareness that naming is itself a form of legibility.

## A Provisional Grammar

What follows is a set of terms that might describe the project's method. They are offered as a working vocabulary, not as a definitive framework.

### 1. The Trace
The trace is what remains — not as a representation, but as an index. It is the mark that survives the hand, the inscription that outlasts its making.

### 2. Legibility
Legibility is the condition of being read. It is not inherent to the trace; it is produced through a relation between the trace and the reader, the archive and the present.

### 3. The Interval
Between the trace and its legibility is an interval — a gap that is not empty but structured. The interval is where reading happens, where meaning is made and unmade.

### 4. The Archive
The archive is the accumulation of traces. But it is also the condition of legibility: it determines what can be read, how, and by whom.

## The Unfinished

The project remains unfinished not because of failure, but because of fidelity — to the questions that cannot be closed, to the traces that resist legibility, to the interval that is the condition of thought.
