    <div class="kicker">The Manifesto</div>
    <h1>On incompletion,<br>and what it means<br>to publish unfinished work.</h1>
    <p class="mf-standfirst">A journal for work that has stopped — and that cannot be restarted without becoming something else. For researchers who have such work, and nowhere to put it. For readers who want to see what research looks like before it is asked to settle.
    </p>
    
  </header>

      <section class="mf-section" id="mf-01">
        <div class="contrib-section-head">
            <div class="kicker">What we publish.</div>
            <h2>Before it is<br>finished.</h2>
        </div>
        <p>
          <em>The Journal of Incomplete Studies</em> publishes research that has stopped short of where publication usually expects it to arrive. Work in progress. Work interrupted or abandoned. Evidence that could not carry the claim it was gathered for. A question that outlived its method. An impasse that was reached and left in place.
        </p>
        <p>
          Think of a fieldwork archive that closed when access did. A corpus that proved too thin for its hypothesis. A translation abandoned halfway because of what the untranslatable passage revealed. A model that failed in a way that taught more than success would have. Each of these ends without becoming a finished study, and each leaves behind something the finished form would not have carried.
        </p>
        <p>
          The journal exists because these materials have nowhere else to go as themselves. They can appear as a preface, a footnote, or a limitations paragraph in the study that did arrive. They rarely appear as work in their own right.
        </p>
        <div class="about-dual">
          <div class="dual-card">
            <div class="dual-col">
              <span class="dual-label">Sense 1</span>
              <span class="dual-title">A journal containing</span>
              <span class="dual-title-sub">incomplete studies</span>
            </div>
            <div class="dual-divider">
              <span class="dual-line"></span>
              <span class="dual-word">also read as</span>
              <span class="dual-line"></span>
            </div>
            <div class="dual-col">
              <span class="dual-label">Sense 2</span>
              <span class="dual-title">A journal studying</span>
              <span class="dual-title-sub">incompleteness itself</span>
            </div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <p>
          Incompletion is not a defect to be corrected. It is a condition with its own weight — and that weight changes what a study can claim, and what a form can carry. The usual form of scholarly publication is not enough, because it is built for studies that have arrived.
        </p>
      </section>
 
      <section class="mf-section" id="mf-02">
        <div class="contrib-section-head">
            <div class="kicker">What completion resolves.</div>
            <h2>What it leaves out.</h2>
        </div>
        <p>
          The conventional research article is a powerful form. It gives readers a method, evidence, an argument and a claim to evaluate. The journal is not opposed to it. It is interested in what the form must resolve in order to exist.
        </p>
        <p>
          A finished study is a settled one. Whatever was uncertain earlier has been resolved, contained or excluded. The method that failed is not the method the article reports. The question that could not be answered is not the question it asks. The evidence that contradicted the conclusion is not the evidence it carries.
        </p>
        <p class="mf-pull">
          The finished study tells us what the research resolved. Incomplete work can also tell us what it could not resolve, and what resolution left behind.
        </p>
        <p>
          This is what makes the finished article useful, and what makes it partial. It hides nothing. It is simply a form designed to hold only what has been settled. Some results are not failures awaiting settlement; they are results that form cannot hold.
        </p>
        <p>
          The journal opens that form. It wants to meet research while its uncertainties are still visible, after a project has stopped, or at the point where the research has itself become the object of doubt: failed procedures, abandoned hypotheses, partial datasets, unresolved interpretations, changed questions, contradictory observations, and records of decisions not to continue.
        </p>
      </section>
 
      <section class="mf-section" id="mf-03">
        <div class="contrib-section-head">
            <div class="kicker">What incompletion produces</div>
            <h2>What the work found instead.</h2>
        </div>
        <p>
          If the finished form cannot carry everything research produces, then what it cannot carry deserves to be read on its own terms. The journal makes two claims, matching the two senses of its title. Incomplete work is worth publishing. And incompletion is worth studying — not because it yields findings that completion would not, but because incompletion is itself a kind of finding.
        </p>
        <p class="mf-pull">
          Incomplete work is not the absence of a finding. It is a finding of a different kind.
        </p>
        <p>
          A failed experiment is not a method that has not yet succeeded. It is a method that has reached its limit, and the limit is what the research found. Missing evidence is not evidence that has not yet been gathered. It is a statement about the conditions under which evidence can exist. An abandoned field site is not a site that was left. It is a record of the arrangements that made the research possible — and of what would have to change for it to be possible again. An argument that will not settle is not an argument that has not yet been made. It is a demonstration that the object resists a single account.
        </p>
        <p>
          This is not the same as the widening of the record that has already occurred. Preprints circulate work before review. Registered reports commit to a design before results are known. Journals for null results publish the finding that nothing was found. Each of these lets more into the record, and each does so on the assumption that a finished study is underneath — a result that happened to be negative, a design that was fixed in advance, a draft that is not yet reviewed but is otherwise complete. The work this journal is concerned with is not waiting for that kind of completion. Its state is the finding, and no further writing would turn it into a conventional article without erasing what made it worth keeping.
        </p>
      </section>

      <section class="mf-section" id="mf-04">
        <div class="contrib-section-head">
            <div class="kicker">What incompletion is</div>
            <h2>States, not stages.</h2>
        </div>
        <p>
          Research does not pass through incompletion on its way to completion. It can stop in a state and stay there. An argument can remain unsettled. Evidence can remain insufficient. A question can outlast the project that produced it.
        </p>
        <p class="mf-states-line">
          <strong>In progress. Provisional. Uncertain. Interrupted. Abandoned. Inaccessible. Partial. Failed. Inconclusive. Unresolved.</strong>
        </p>
        <p>
          These are states knowledge can be in — not temporary conditions waiting to be corrected. When a study stops, what it leaves is not a lesser version of a finished study. It is a study in a different state, carrying things the finished form cannot. The states can appear in different parts of a study. Each kind of incompletion carries something different:
        </p>
        <div class="mf-types">

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Conceptual</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">An argument has not settled, or competing interpretations remain in play.</span>
          </div>

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Empirical</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">Evidence is partial, contradictory, inaccessible, or insufficient for the claim it was gathered for.</span>
          </div>

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Methodological</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">A method has failed, produced unexpected results, or revealed a problem that changes the research.</span>
          </div>

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Historical</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">A record was lost, destroyed, or never existed, and the research cannot proceed without it.</span>
          </div>

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Formal</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">The work does not yet have a stable shape, or deliberately refuses one.</span>
          </div>

          <div class="mf-type">
            <span class="mf-type-label">Question-based</span>
            <span class="mf-type-desc">The research clarifies a problem without resolving it, leaving the question itself as the result.</span>
          </div>

        </div>
        <p>
          Some studies here may continue elsewhere. Some may become conventional publications. Others may stay unresolved for good. The journal asks for no prediction — only that the state of the work be described clearly enough to be read.
        </p>
      </section>
 
            <section class="mf-section" id="mf-05">
        <div class="contrib-section-head">
            <div class="kicker">What the writing takes</div>
            <h2>The shape of a state.</h2>
        </div>
        <p>
          Incompletion can be an alibi. An unfinished draft can pass itself off as a considered position, and the vocabulary of provisionality can excuse almost anything. The journal's answer is not to lower its standard but to change what the standard measures.
        </p>
        <p class="mf-pull">
          Not whether the work is finished, but whether its form fits its state.
        </p>
        <p>
          That puts the measure in the writing, and scholars are trained to write against it. Scholarly prose closes in at least three ways at once: rhetorically, by ending on a claim; epistemically, by presenting the question as settled; socially, by reaching a point where the work can be handed off and cited. The journal has no quarrel with these in themselves. Its quarrel is with their being applied to work that has not earned them. In an article that closes, the doubts and qualifications that once surrounded each statement drop away as it hardens into a result. Writing that does not close keeps them in place, attached to what they qualify.
        </p>
        <p>
          This is not the same as confession. A candid account of how the research went is still told from the end, where every difficulty has a place and a purpose. It resolves the work retroactively and turns a state into a stage. The writing asked for here refuses that arrangement. The abandoned hypothesis stays abandoned. The changed question stays visibly changed. The failed method is not recast as a step toward the method that worked. No account is a transcript of a state, but an account can decline the one story most research writing tells without noticing: that everything that happened was heading toward what was found.
        </p>
        <p>
          Nor is it a matter of style. A fragment can be as closed as an essay, and an ending that gestures at the unfinished is still an ending. The journal asks for something plainer and harder: that the writing say, in terms a stranger can check, where the work stands, what stopped it, and what a reader can and cannot build on. An atmosphere of unfinishedness is not information.
        </p>
        <p>
          What shape this takes is not settled in advance. The article closes by design and the research note by abbreviation. Even the essay, the form most at home with doubt, tends to end by gathering itself up. Writing that holds a state has no ready-made form, and takes its shape from the state: from a thinking that has not stopped, an argument that has not settled, a question that has outlasted the attempt to answer it. The measure is fit, not display. A form fits when it tells the reader something true about where the work stands, and fails when it only resembles unfinishedness. There is no template. There is only the refusal of an ending the subject has not reached.
        </p>
      </section>
